NEXUSENGINE FIELD GUIDE / PRODUCTION

Captions that work with the picture.

Review subtitle meaning, readability, timing, and export choices together.

01

Review the text against the source

Start with meaning. Correct speech recognition errors before translating them, and compare text extracted from the picture against the original frame. Check names, numbers, prices, and units separately; they are easy to overlook while reading for fluency.

Automatic transcription is a starting point for review. Whisper is a speech recognition model, not a product-fact verification system. Listen to unclear segments and mark uncertainty instead of turning a plausible guess into a confident statement.

02

Check readability in the actual frame

Preview captions over the moving video, not just in a text editor. Look for low contrast, text covering a product detail, awkward line breaks, or a background that makes the words disappear. Use a smaller screen as part of the review because a layout that feels comfortable on a desktop can be crowded on a phone.

Keep related words together where possible. Test long product names and the widest lines in the target language. Avoid applying a universal character limit without considering the script, font, video size, and audience.

A useful checkpoint

Can a viewer read this line without losing the product action behind it? If not, revise the wording, placement, timing, or shot rather than shrinking the text until it becomes difficult to read.

03

Inspect the beginning and end of each line

Move through caption boundaries and watch the action they describe. Make sure a line appears when its meaning becomes relevant and does not disappear before it can be read. Check whether consecutive lines flicker or overlap, particularly after editing the translation.

Listen and watch together. A caption may look correct when paused but feel out of sync during playback. Recheck timing after changing voiceover pacing or any edit that changes the video duration.

04

Choose what the export should contain

A separate subtitle file keeps text separate from the picture and may allow a viewer or platform to select a track. Burned-in subtitles become part of the video image. FFmpeg provides a subtitles filter for rendering subtitle text into video frames.

Choose the format for the destination and test the uploaded result when possible. Keep an editable subtitle copy even if the final delivery uses burned-in text. That makes later language corrections easier to track.

For accessibility, captions may need speaker identification and relevant non-speech sound information in addition to dialogue. Translated speech subtitles and complete accessibility captions should not be treated as interchangeable deliverables.

05

Sign off the file you will actually share

Watch the exported file from beginning to end. Check the first and last captions, transitions, audio, and whether the correct language version was selected. File existence does not establish content quality.

  • Source text and product facts have been checked.
  • Target wording has been reviewed by a suitable language reviewer.
  • Caption placement, contrast, and line breaks work on a small screen.
  • Timing remains correct after voiceover and video edits.
  • The delivery format and final exported file have been verified.

NexusEngine offers subtitle editing and caption styling in the desktop prototype. The checklist above is a suggested human review process, not an automated guarantee.
